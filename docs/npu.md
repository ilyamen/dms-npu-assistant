# Running a 4B model on the Intel NPU 3720

Notes from getting Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 to answer at a usable speed on the NPU of a Core Ultra 7 265K (Arrow Lake, NPU 3720, same NPU as Meteor Lake), with OpenVINO GenAI 2026.4 and NPU driver 1.38.

## Results

Decode speed after the first token, 16 held-out Russian prompts, DDR5-6400:

| tok/s | model alone | with the Russian EAGLE-3 head |
|---|---|---|
| CPU (OpenVINO) | 35.4 | **49.1** (chain, k=3) |
| iGPU (Arc, Xe-LPG) | 25.0 | 27.9 (tree k5/d3/b2) |
| NPU, stock compiler | 17.2 | 23.2 |
| NPU, patched compiler | **22.2–23.0** | 24.1–25.5 |

The plugin uses the bottom-left and top-left cells: the NPU without the head as the always-on assistant, the CPU with the head as the ⚡ boost.

## 1. Our own INT4 build

The stock INT4 exports were slower on the NPU and worse on Russian, so the model is compressed from the FP16 OpenVINO IR with NNCF:

- `INT4_SYM`, channel-wise (`group_size=-1`), `ratio=1.0`, `backup_mode=INT8_SYM`;
- `all_layers=True`, so the embedding and lm_head are INT4 too: about +25% on the NPU;
- data-aware AWQ + Scale Estimation on 64 × 256-token samples (Russian technical answers and English docs).

A 4B build takes 11–14 minutes and about 18 GB of RAM. `all_layers` is not safe for every model: Phi-4-mini, with its 200k vocabulary, produces garbage with it. Each new model gets checked in FP16 on the CPU first.

## 2. The npu_compiler CMX patch (+31%)

Upstream issue: [openvinotoolkit/npu_compiler#364](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/npu_compiler/issues/364).

In decode, the NPU spent ~16 ms per token with the DPU busy and DMA idle. A per-task hardware timeline showed why. The weights of the big MatMuls (gate/up/down) are tiled, and the next tile's weights should load while the DPU works on the current one (double buffering). They didn't. Each tile was loaded only after the previous one had finished.

The cause was the memory allocator, not the data flow. A 40 KB attention buffer, whose size depends on the KV length, sat in the middle of the 1.94 MB CMX. That split the free space into 1.25 MB and 643 KB, so there was no room for a second 778 KB weight tile. The best-fit, bottom-up allocator put tile k+1 at the address tile k had just freed. That created a write-after-read dependency, a barrier, and serialized execution. The cost model didn't object: VPUNN has no cost for INT4 FC on VPU 2.7, so 2304 DPU tasks were costed at 0.

The patch adds two options to the `feasible-allocation` pass, both off by default:

- `cmx-small-buffer-top-threshold=<bytes>` allocates small buffers from the top of CMX, which keeps the middle contiguous for weight tiles;
- `weights-ping-pong=true` keeps the next big buffer off the range the last compute task just freed.

With `cmx-small-buffer-top-threshold=65536 weights-ping-pong=true`, a token takes 43.5 ms instead of 57.0 ms (17.5 → 23.0 tok/s). The output is bit-identical.

The plugin runs the patched compiler without touching the system. The backend starts with `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` pointing at a release build of the compiler (`/var/lib/ollama-models/npu-dev/cid-rel`), with `ZE_INTEL_NPU_CACHE_SIZE=0` so the driver doesn't reuse blobs from the stock compiler, and with its own `CACHE_DIR`.

What can't be fixed from our side: the 3720 has no fused SDPA kernel. Every small non-matrix op (norms, RoPE, softmax, converts) costs 8–14 µs to launch on SHAVE, against ~1.5 µs on NPU 4000. Intel's 2025–2026 attention work (`sdpa`, `flash_sdpa`, `rms_norm` kernels) targets NPU 4000 and newer only. The kernel sources and toolchain are closed, so we can't write our own.

## 3. Why the EAGLE head isn't used on the NPU

We trained our own EAGLE-3 draft head for 2507 on Russian technical dialogues: 8961 questions, answers generated by the same INT4 build. The public head's 32k draft vocabulary has only 11 Cyrillic tokens and accepts 20–25% of guesses on Russian. Ours has 2655 Cyrillic tokens and accepts 0.72 / 0.68 / … / 0.63 over draft steps 1–6. On the stock compiler, it took the NPU from 15.8 to 21.9 tok/s (+39%).

After the compiler patch, the head adds only ~8%:

- a speculative step costs ~80 ms to verify (an 8-token graph) and ~35 ms to draft (4 head calls of ~9 ms);
- at 43.5 ms per plain token, the head pays off only above (80 + 35) / 43.5 ≈ 2.65 tokens per step, and it gets 3.05;
- the rest of the verify cost is attention over 8 queries × 1280 KV: KV reads, softmax on SHAVE, and no fused SDPA on the 3720. The allocator patch doesn't help there.

So the NPU runs the model alone (23 tok/s, simpler and lighter). The head goes where it pays: on the CPU it takes 35.4 to 49.1 tok/s, and that's the ⚡ boost. Real answers come out at 36–47 tok/s, more for short ones.
